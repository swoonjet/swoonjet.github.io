How it works

The land at the start is the reconstruction of Pangaea at 250 million years ago from the Müller et al. (2019) plate model, served by the GPlates Web Service. The real history of the same model is kept beside it for comparison.

Each plate is a rigid cap of crust on a sphere that turns about its own Euler pole. Where two plates overlap, continental crust rides over ocean floor, which sinks and builds volcanic arcs; two continents crumple into mountains and slow each other down. Where they separate, a rift stretches the margin and then makes new ocean floor, which gets deeper as it ages. Hotspots fixed in the mantle leave island chains. Climate, rain shadows and vegetation are worked out afterwards from latitude, height and the distance moist air has travelled.

It is a simplified model for speculation, not a forecast. Plate speeds are set by you, not computed from mantle forces, so the same rifts can be made to run in any direction.
