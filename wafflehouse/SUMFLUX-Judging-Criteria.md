# SUMFLUX — Waffle House Contest: Judging Criteria

**The brief:** Each story must involve a waffle house, or the *idea* of a waffle house.

---

## 1. Shortlisting

The goal is to narrow the entries down to a manageable shortlist before anyone does detailed scoring.

- **Two reads per story.** The reading is arranged so every story is seen by **at least two judges**, not one. Two reads stop a single judge's off-day from sinking a good entry.
- **Use a simple gate here.** For each story, each reader marks one of:
  - **Advance** — yes, this deserves full scoring
  - **Maybe** — on the fence
  - **Cut** — no

  …plus **one sentence** saying why.
- **Resolve into a shortlist:**
  - Two *Advance* → goes through
  - Two *Cut* → out
  - Any split (Advance/Maybe, Maybe/Cut, etc.) → quick group look to decide
- **Target shortlist: 8–10 stories.** That's the set that gets the full weighted scoring below — scored by all judges.

---

## 2. The Scoring Matrix

Only shortlisted stories get scored this way. Four criteria, each scored **1–100**, then multiplied by its weight. Weights sum to 100%.

| # | Criterion | What we're rewarding | Weight |
|---|-----------|----------------------|--------|
| 1 | **Craft / Writing Quality** | Prose, control of language, structure, pacing, an ending that lands | **35%** |
| 2 | **Idea & Originality** | Freshness of concept, surprise, a take on the brief we haven't seen ten times | **30%** |
| 3 | **Brief Fit — Waffle House** | How meaningfully the waffle house (literal *or* as idea/atmosphere/metaphor) is woven in — not just name-dropped | **20%** |
| 4 | **Overall Impact** | The gut score: did it move you, stay with you, would you publish it? | **15%** |

**Weighted total = Σ (score × weight)** → a final number out of 100.

> **Judges only enter a 1–100 for each criterion.** Each judge's scorecard works out that judge's weighted total automatically; a separate master tally sheet then averages the judges' totals for the final score — no one does the math by hand.

**Worked example** — a story scoring 80 craft, 90 idea, 60 brief, 90 impact:

`(80 × .35) + (90 × .30) + (60 × .20) + (90 × .15) = 28 + 27 + 12 + 13.5 = 80.5 / 100`

### Keeping scoring consistent

**Anchor the 1–100 scale** so a "75" means the same thing to everyone:

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 90–100 | Exceptional — publish as-is |
| 70–89 | Strong |
| 50–69 | Solid but flawed |
| 30–49 | Notable problems |
| 1–29 | Doesn't work |

- **Final score per story = the average of all judges' weighted totals.** No scores are dropped; they're averaged.
- **Brief Fit is a gate as well as a score:** any story scoring **30 or below** on criterion 3 is **disqualified** regardless of its total. The prompt is the point — a story that doesn't really answer it shouldn't win on craft alone.

---

## 3. Tiebreakers

If two stories finish with the same final score, work down this list until the tie breaks:

1. **Higher Craft score** (criterion 1) — reward the better-written piece.
2. **Higher Idea & Originality score** (criterion 2) — then the more original.
3. **Ranked head-to-head vote.** Each judge ranks the tied stories (1st, 2nd, 3rd…); lowest total ranking wins. A ranked vote can itself end in a tie — in that case the lead/editor casts the deciding vote.
